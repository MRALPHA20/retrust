R↗ ReTrust

Open-source OT risk tooling

Know the cyber risk of a PLC before you buy it.

ReTrust scores industrial hardware — PLCs, switches, controllers — for cyber risk from public advisory data, and shows the evidence behind every number.

The one question it answers

“Of these candidate products, which one should we put on the purchase order?”

It is decision support for the moment before you buy — a shortlist review, not a live scanner. It never touches the device; it reads what the world already knows about that model and turns it into a verdict you can defend.

How it works

You give it a product. It resolves exactly what you mean, checks the deal-breakers first, then weighs the evidence into one verdict.

  1. Identify the hardware.

    Give a manufacturer order number, or a vendor and model. It resolves the exact product in a local index built from public sources — or it abstains rather than guess.

  2. Check the deal-breakers.

    Hard gates fire first: a known-exploited vulnerability, a ransomware link, an unfixable critical flaw, or a sanctions / covered-list match. Any one of these means AVOID, and no good score elsewhere can average it away.

  3. Weigh the evidence.

    If it clears the gates, four pillars multiply into a 0–100 score. Evidence quality is reported separately — it never flatters the number.

  4. Read the verdict.

    PREFER, CONDITIONAL, AVOID or NOT ASSESSED — each shown with the pillars, the CVEs, and any gate behind it. Never a bare number.

What the verdict means

PREFERClears every gate and scores well. The stronger candidate on the evidence.
CONDITIONALUsable, but with a caution a human should look at before signing.
AVOIDA hard gate tripped, or the score is poor. Keep it off the purchase order.
NOT ASSESSEDCouldn’t be resolved or lacks evidence. “Needs review” — never read as safe.

What’s inside the score

Four weighted pillars, each reading only public evidence. Raw CVE counts are never scored — that would punish a vendor for disclosing honestly.

🎯 Exposure 60%

How dangerous are the actual flaws, on this box? Threat (is it being exploited?) × reachability × impact × whether it still applies — worked out per flaw, then concentrated on the worst few rather than averaged.

🔧 Patchability

Can you fix it? Whether real fixes have shipped for the affected versions.

🏢 Vendor practice

Does the maker disclose and document well? A proxy for security maturity.

📜 Certification

Independent assurance — IEC 62443 / Common Criteria — counted only when it helps.

🌐 Network exposure optional

How the box sits in your plant (isolated vs internet-facing). You supply it; it’s never assumed.

How the score is calculated

The pillars combine into one 0–100 number by a weighted product — but that is only ever the middle layer. A verdict is three layers:

1
Hard gates — pass / fail, outside the maths. Any one tripping → AVOID.
2
Weighted score — the 0–100 average of the pillars that had evidence.
3
Bands — the number becomes a word: ≥ 70 PREFER · ≥ 40 CONDITIONAL · else AVOID.
S  =  E0.60  ×  P0.20  ×  V0.15  ×  C0.05

A product, not an average, so dangerous exposure cannot be bought back with a good vendor record and a certificate — the same rule the gates enforce, applied to the weighted part.

Worked on the real ABB AC 800M result — each pillar raised to its exponent, then multiplied together:

PillarExponentScoreFactor
E — Exposure0.6082.214.10
P — Patch availability0.2052.02.20
V — Vendor practice0.15100.02.00
C — Certification0.05100.01.26
multiplied together78.0

14.10 × 2.20 × 2.00 × 1.26 = 78.0. The factors multiply — each pillar contributes scoreexponent. Pillars enter as 1–100, never 0, so one unmeasured term cannot annihilate the result; and a pillar with no evidence at all is dropped and the remaining exponents renormalised, never scored as a neutral 50. Two rules override the maths: a certificate may only lift the score (never drag it down), and an unfixable hardcoded-credential flaw caps it just below PREFER whatever the number says.

What you tell it about your plant

Five optional inputs describe your situation rather than the product. The division of labour is the whole design rule: ReTrust supplies the vulnerability intelligence; you supply the facts about your plant. It never asks you to score a vendor or guess a percentage.

Buying, or already own it?

On a purchase, a fix that exists is leverage you can require in the order. On an installed asset it changes nothing until somebody actually applies it.

What would a compromise cost?

Safety or environmental harm, loss of process control, recoverable disruption, minor, or negligible. This is the one thing only you know.

Can the flaw actually be reached?

Where the box sits, and whether you have verified the vulnerable interface is blocked. An unverified claim doesn’t count.

Is the fix on the box?

Applied, mitigated, merely available, or unknown. “A fix exists” and “the fix is installed” are different facts and don’t score alike.

Every answer can only ever narrow the risk. Saying nothing is already the cautious reading, so a claim about your own plant never makes a flaw look worse than its own evidence says — and an unanswered question stays unresolved, never quietly set to a best or worst case.

Each assessment also prints a short vector recording every choice, so somebody who wasn’t in the room can re-run and dispute it — the idea borrowed from CVSS:

RT1/AT:installed/FW:3.1.6/NX:it_ot/IF:blocked/OC:control/RS:applied/DS:2026-10-16

Why network exposure is optional

This is the one thing the tool refuses to guess. It describes your plant, not the product — and ReTrust never scans your network. Leaving it out does not assume the best (isolated): silence is read as full reachability, the conservative case, and the product is judged on its own evidence. Declare it and it narrows reachability inside Exposure — it is an input to the A term rather than a pillar of its own, because “an attacker cannot route here” is the same statement reachability already makes, and scoring it twice would double one declared fact. The three choices describe where the box lives:

OptionReachWhat it means
Isolated×0.3No network route from office IT or the internet into OT — air-gapped or fully segmented. Reaching it needs local access.
IT–OT×0.7OT joined to enterprise IT across a firewall / DMZ. Not internet-facing, but a compromised office machine could pivot toward it.
Internet–OT×1.0A routed path from the internet can reach OT — directly, or via a remote-access gateway, VPN or cellular link.

The same PLC can be any of these depending on where you install it — so the risk shifts with the deployment, not the part number. Internet–OT is the no-narrowing case, which is exactly what silence already assumed. Declaring it moves the score; on the same ABB box:

Network exposure you declareScore
not supplied (the conservative reading)78.0
isolated84.9
IT–OT81.0
internet–OT78.0

Isolation only nudges the number. It can never rescue a gate or a cap.

  • A gated part stays AVOID, however isolated. The exploited Siemens S7-1500 rises 51.3 → 79.3 when you declare “isolated” — well into PREFER territory on the number alone — but the known-exploited gate still forces AVOID — a CVE being attacked in the wild isn’t un-attacked by your topology, and an air-gap erodes over a 20-year asset life.
  • A capped part stays capped. The Modicon M340 sits at 69.9 with or without isolation: a hardcoded credential with no fix can’t be isolated into being preferable.

Why you can trust it

  • Hard gates sit outside the score. A known-exploited hit or a sanctions match can’t be averaged away.
  • Raw CVE counts are never scored. A diligent discloser shouldn’t rank below a silent one.
  • No resolution, no score. An unknown product gets “needs review”, never a flattering 100.
  • Every number shows its evidence, and the inputs are signature-checked back to CISA.

Run it yourself

Python 3.10+, no dependencies. Works offline once the public sources are fetched.

# clone and build the local index from public sources git clone …/retrust && cd retrust python3 -m retrust.cli setup # open the browser workspace, or score from the terminal python3 -m retrust.cli serve python3 -m retrust.cli score --order-number 6ES7518-4AX00-1AB0 --version 3.1.6
