The scoring, in full

How ReTrust turns evidence into a verdict.

Everything you need to explain the score: the formula, every pillar’s maths, the gates, and — for each — why it’s built that way.

1 · The shape: three layers, not one number

A single blended number is dangerous for procurement, because a genuine deal-breaker can be averaged away by good scores elsewhere. So a verdict is built in three separate stages:

1
Hard gates — pass / fail, outside the maths. Any one tripping ends it: AVOID, no score.
2
Weighted score — a 0–100 product of four pillars, for everything that clears the gates.
3
Bands — the number becomes a word: ≥ 70 PREFER · ≥ 40 CONDITIONAL · else AVOID.

The one line that captures the philosophy: a deal-breaker can’t be out-voted by a decent average.

2 · The formula

The composite is a weighted product of the pillar scores — not an average — and only of the pillars that actually had evidence:

S  =  E0.60  ×  P0.20  ×  V0.15  ×  C0.05

Why a product and not a mean? So that dangerous exposure cannot be bought back with a good vendor record and a certificate. In an average, 60% weight still lets the other 40% drag a bad score up; in a product, a poor E pulls the whole result down. It is the same rule the hard gates enforce, applied to the weighted part.

Two details that matter. Pillars enter as 1–100, never 0: a zero in a product is absorbing, and one unmeasured term must not erase every other measurement — the floor keeps a catastrophic pillar catastrophic (E = 0 lands near 5/100, deep in AVOID) without annihilating the score. And a pillar with no evidence is dropped and the remaining exponents renormalised back to 1.0 — never scored as a neutral 50, which would quietly shift every ranking. Each pillar is 0–100 where higher is better.

3 · The weights, and why these

Weight = how much a pillar counts. They sum to 1.0 and are ordered by decision relevance:

PillarExponentWhy it sits there
E — Exposure0.60Three-fifths of the result. It carries the whole question of “how dangerous are the actual flaws, here, on this box?” — threat, reachability, impact and applicability combined.
P — Patch availability0.20Can you fix what is wrong? The maintenance half of the decision.
V — Vendor practice0.15A softer, proxy signal, so it gets less pull.
C — Certification0.05The lightest, and the only asymmetric one: it may lift a score, never lower it.

In a product the exponent is leverage rather than a slice of points: raising E does more for the result than raising C by the same amount, and a bad E costs more than a good C can recover. The honest part: these are a defensible default, not a claim of truth. They live in a config file you can change, and retrust sensitivity re-runs the verdict under hundreds of random reweightings — if a verdict only holds under this exact weighting, it is flagged as a judgement call.

4 · Each pillar: the maths, and why that shape

Every pillar returns a 0–100 score. Here is exactly how each is computed, and the reason it isn’t simpler.

🎯 E — Exposure

exponent 0.60
z = T' × A × H × U per finding, 0–1, higher is worse T' = 0.40 + 0.60 T threat amplifies; it never zeroes the rest R = 0.70 z₁ + 0.20 z₂ + 0.10 z₃ + 0.10 mean(the rest) E = 100 (1 − R)

Built per flaw, then concentrated on the worst few. Averaging is what a purchase decision least wants: a product with one catastrophic flaw and forty minor ones should not come out like forty-one middling ones. So each finding gets its own risk number and the worst three decide most of the result.

T' — threat from KEV, ransomware linkage and EPSS. Mapped onto [0.40, 1] rather than used raw, because the OT median EPSS is 0.007 — multiply by that directly and a critical, reachable, unfixed flaw scores as harmless purely because nobody has attacked it yet. A — reachability from the CVSS attack vector, narrowed by where the box sits and by a verified interface block. H — impact is the flaw’s own CVSS severity scaled by the process consequence you declare. U — applicability asks whether it still applies to the firmware you are buying or running.

The tail term is ours, not the proposal’s. Top-three alone cannot tell three severe flaws from three hundred — they give an identical result. So a long tail of equally bad findings still moves the number, bounded at a tenth so breadth can never outvote the worst case.

🔧 P — Patch availability

exponent 0.20
P = 100 × (fixed + 0.4 × workaround) / known known = fixed + workaround + none_available (only CVEs whose remedy is stated)

Why 0.4 for a workaround: a mitigation is not a fix, but it is not nothing — it earns part credit. Only CVEs whose remediation status is actually stated are counted, so “no data” is never read as “no fix”. Some vendors file a shipped patch under “mitigation” with the fix named in prose; the tool reads that text, so a fixed product is not shown as 0/10 fixed.

🏢 V — Vendor practice

exponent 0.15
V = 70 × quality + (30 if the vendor runs its own CSAF feed) quality = ( share_with_CVSS + share_with_CWE ) / 2

Why the +30 is the real signal: advisory content is nearly constant on CISA’s corpus, because CISA normalises what it republishes — scoring it alone handed free points to everyone. What genuinely varies, and is the vendor’s own doing, is whether they publish machine-readable advisories themselves. Not being on the publisher list is never a penalty, only a missed bonus.

📜 C — Certification

exponent 0.05
C = 50 + 15 (certified process, 62443-4-1) + 20 (certified component, 62443-4-2) + 15 (a cert covers this product specifically) S = max( S_with_C , S_without_C ) it may lift, never lower

Positive evidence only, and the review got this one wrong. Applied naively, C^0.05 costs an uncertified product 20.6% of its score (57.8 against 72.7, measured) — which punishes the majority of OT hardware for a vendor’s paperwork decision, since most is never submitted. So absence is excluded rather than penalised, and the composite is computed with and without the certificate and the better one taken. The pillar meant to reward certification is never allowed to punish its absence.

📊 Evidence quality — reported, never scored

exponent 0
quality = ( share_decided + share_with_CVSS + share_with_EPSS ) / 3

Deliberately kept out of the risk number. It answers a different question — “how much should you trust the score above?” — so it sits beside the verdict but never inside it. A product whose version ranges could not be confirmed scores low here even if it looks clean; the right response is review, not confidence. Exploitation and severity are also shown beside the score as diagnostics, since they now feed Exposure rather than scoring separately.

4b · The facts only you have

Four inputs describe your plant, not the product. ReTrust supplies the vulnerability intelligence and never asks you to score a vendor or guess a percentage. Every one is optional, and every one can only ever narrow the risk — saying nothing is already the conservative reading, so a claim about your own plant never makes a flaw worse than its own vector says.

InputChoicesWhat it changes
Assessment typepurchase · installedRecords whether this is a procurement decision or an existing asset review
Network exposureisolated · IT–OT · internet–OTNarrows A. Folded into reachability rather than scored separately — “an attacker cannot route here” is the statement A already makes
Process consequencesafety · control · recoverable · minor · negligibleScales H. Left blank, exposure is reported at full process impact
Interface reachable?exposed · blocked · unknownOnly a verified block narrows A
Fix / mitigation statusapplied · mitigated · available · planned · unknownApply only to all in-scope findings; planned work does not reduce current risk

Each assessment also carries a reproducible vector, so a result can be re-run and disputed by somebody who was not in the room — CVSS’s idea, and a good one:

RT2/AT:installed/FW:3.1.6/NX:it_ot/IF:blocked/OC:control/RS:applied/DS:4f3a9c2d6b18

The firmware and operator choices are recorded with each result. DS fingerprints the recorded source, count and capture-date metadata; it is not a checksum of the full advisory database.

5 · The gates (outside the maths — any one → AVOID)

These are facts that end the assessment. They’re checked on the product’s own (first-party) findings, and no weighted score can out-vote them.

Known-exploited vulnerability
A CVE appears in CISA’s KEV catalogue — confirmed exploitation in the wild.
Ransomware-linked
A CVE tied to a known ransomware campaign.
Critical with no fix
CVSS ≥ 9.0, high exploitation likelihood (EPSS ≥ 0.5 or in KEV), and no vendor remedy — critical, reachable, and nothing the buyer can do.
Supplier screening
The vendor is on a sanctions / covered list. Checked on the name alone, so it fires even if the product isn’t in the advisory corpus.

A KEV that arrives inside a third-party component bundle (e.g. the Linux stack a device ships) is shown as a caution, not a gate — the bundle proves the component ships, not that each of its hundreds of CVEs is reachable on the device.

6 · Two overrides on the average

Certification clamp

composite = max( composite , composite_without_certification )

Because the certification pillar may only reward, it’s never allowed to pull the number down — otherwise a vendor holding a mid-tier certificate could score below one holding none, and the pillar meant to credit certification would punish it.

Unmitigable cap

if a hardcoded-credential-class flaw has no fix and the composite would reach PREFER → composite = PREFER threshold − 0.1 (69.9 by default)

A credential baked into firmware with no shipped fix isn’t a weighting question: whatever the other pillars say, the operator can’t engineer around it for the life of the asset, so the product cannot be preferred while that stands.

7 · Bands → verdict

score ≥ 70
PREFER
40 – 69
CONDITIONAL
< 40, or any gate
AVOID

A fourth outcome, NOT ASSESSED, means the product couldn’t be resolved or had no evidence — “needs review”, never read as a clean 100.

8 · Worked examples

① A clean product — ABB AC 800M → PREFER 77.9

E — Exposure0.6082.114.08

V — Vendor practice0.15100.02.00

14.08 × 2.20 × 2.00 × 1.26 = 77.9 → ≥ 70 → PREFER. Note the factors multiply, they do not add: each pillar contributes scoreexponent. Patch availability at 52 is what holds this back — fixing it alone would gain about 11 points, which is exactly what retrust sensitivity prints.

② A gated product — Siemens S7-1500 (fw 3.1.6) → AVOID 50.9

Its weighted score is 50.9 — CONDITIONAL territory. But a known-exploited gate fired, so the verdict is AVOID regardless. This is the whole point: the gate cannot be out-voted by a decent score.

③ An unmitigable weakness — Modicon M340 → CONDITIONAL 64.5

It carries a hardcoded-credential CVE with no fix — a credential the operator cannot change for the life of the asset. Exposure already scores it below PREFER, so the unmitigable cap has nothing to do here; were the other pillars strong enough to reach 70, the cap would pin it just below. Either way the invariant holds, and across the corpus none of the 497 products carrying such a flaw reads PREFER. Isolation does not move it: you cannot isolate a baked-in credential into being preferable.

④ The one that looks wrong — S7-1500 MFP, 1,034 CVEs → CONDITIONAL 55.5

A box with 1,034 CVEs scoring mid-table rather than bottom looks like a failure until you see why: all but a handful arrive in third-party component-bundle advisories — the Linux and OpenSSL stack the device ships — and a bundle establishes that a component is present, not that each of its CVEs is reachable on the controller. So exposure measures the vendor’s own firmware, and the bundled KEV is raised as a caution rather than a gate, because gating on it would punish precisely the vendors who publish their component stacks. The caution is the output here, not a footnote. Read it aloud in a demo.

9 · Things to get right when you explain it

  • A high score is good. 82/100 means low risk, buy with confidence — not “82% risky.” The tool measures how safe a product looks.
  • Gates sit outside the score. KEV, ransomware, unfixable-critical and sanctions can’t be averaged away.
  • Raw CVE counts are never scored. A diligent discloser shouldn’t rank below a vendor who stays quiet.
  • No resolution, no score. An unknown product is NOT ASSESSED — never a flattering 100.
  • “Couldn’t parse the version range” stays in scope. Uncertainty is flagged for review, never assumed safe.
  • Evidence quality is separate from risk. It tells you how far to trust the number, not how risky the product is.
  • The weights are configurable, and tested. retrust sensitivity shows whether a verdict survives different weightings; a fragile one is labelled a judgement call.
