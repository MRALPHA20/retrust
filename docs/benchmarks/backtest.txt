Scoring every product on advisories published before 2022-01-01,
then measuring against what was disclosed after it.

BACK-TEST AT 2022-01-01
  products scored on pre-cutoff data only     5,848

  correlation                                rho
  score vs CVEs after the cutoff          +0.484   p=0.001 (1,000 shuffles)
  score vs CVEs before the cutoff         +0.156   (is the score just re-reading history?)
  CVEs before vs CVEs after               +0.184   (how much does volume persist?)

  verdict at cutoff    products  median    mean  any later  KEV after
  AVOID                      25       0    0.12        4%          0

  5823/5848 assessments need verification. Historical advisories do not establish site reachability or OT consequences. Correlations use conservative score endpoints, not completed procurement assessments; they do not validate RT4 deployment decisions.
  A higher score went with MORE later CVEs (rho = +0.484), which is the opposite of what a risk score should do if CVE count were the right yardstick. It is not: the score rewards vendors who run a real disclosure programme, and those vendors publish more CVEs. One Siemens CPU here had 2 CVEs before the cutoff and over a thousand after, because a single advisory bundled hundreds of upstream Linux CVEs.
  The score is not merely re-reading past volume: its correlation with CVEs already known at the cutoff is +0.156, while past volume predicts future volume at +0.184.
  Confirmed exploitation would be the sound outcome to test, but only 2 product(s) across 2 vendor(s) later appeared in KEV. That is too few, and too concentrated, to support a rate, so no such claim is made here.
  This is observational. Widely deployed equipment attracts more researchers and so accumulates more CVEs however well it was built, and a product discontinued after the cutoff collects none and looks clean.

  Even within a vendor, a higher score went with slightly more later CVEs (rho = +0.340). The magnitude is the point: holding the vendor constant collapsed the effect, so most of the pooled correlation was a vendor effect rather than anything about the score. What remains is product-level exposure -- a flagship PLC draws more research than a niche sensor from the same catalogue.
  With 3,796 products the p-value is small for an effect this size; significance here means 'not zero', not 'large enough to act on'. The split of 2 vendors negative against 17 positive says the direction is not consistent between manufacturers.
  Dropping Siemens changes materially the result (+0.340 to +0.110). That vendor bundles hundreds of upstream CVEs into single advisories, so it dominates any count-based outcome. Treat the pooled number as describing that vendor more than the score.
  Counting CVEs still measures disclosure volume; this design removes the vendor-level part of that, not the product-level part.
